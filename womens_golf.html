Championship Format

Regional Competition

Six 54-hole regional competitions will determine participants for the championships finals:

  • 12 teams and 6 individuals compete at each regional
  • The low 5 teams and the low individual not on those teams advance to finals
  • Regional tournaments are preliminary rounds of NCAA championships competition
2025 Regional Sites:
  • Columbus, Ohio – OSU Golf Club
  • Charlottesville, Virginia – Birdwood Golf Course
  • Gold Canyon, Arizona – Superstition Mountain Golf and Country Club
  • Lexington, Kentucky – Keene Trace Golf Club
  • Lubbock, Texas – The Rawls Course
  • Norman, Oklahoma – Jimmie Austin OU Golf Club

Finals Competition

All 30 teams and 6 individuals compete in the finals at Omni La Costa Resort & Spa:

Rounds 1-3 (54 holes): All teams and individuals compete in stroke play

Round 4 (72 holes total):

  • Top 15 teams advance
  • Top 9 individuals not on an advancing team advance
  • After 72 holes, the individual champion is crowned
  • Top 8 teams advance to match play

Match Play (Team Competition):

  • Quarterfinals, Semifinals, and Finals
  • 5 individual matches per team match
  • First team to win 3 points advances
  • Seeding: #1 vs #8, #2 vs #7, #3 vs #6, #4 vs #5

Player Substitutions

Prior to a stroke or match play round, a team may substitute a sixth player for any member, provided:

  • The committee is notified at least 10 minutes prior to starting time
  • The sixth player is an eligible member of the institution's team
  • Only one substitution per round is allowed

Selection Criteria & Process

Championship Field

  • 72 teams (including automatic qualifiers)
  • 36 individuals not on those teams (including automatic qualifiers)
  • Distributed across six regional sites (12 teams + 6 individuals per regional)

Automatic Qualification

29 conferences receive automatic qualification for the 2025 regional championships, including:

  • American Athletic Conference
  • Atlantic Coast Conference
  • Big 12 Conference
  • Big Ten Conference
  • Southeastern Conference
  • And 24 additional conferences

Conference Individual Medalists: If not selected on a participating team, conference individual medalists receive an automatic invitation as an individual. If there is a tie for individual medalist at the conference championship, a playoff must be held to determine the automatic qualifier.

At-Large Selection

The remaining 43 teams are selected at large nationally based on selection criteria.

Primary Selection Criteria (in priority order)

  1. National Collegiate Golf ranking
  2. Head-to-head results
  3. Head-to-head stroke differential
  4. Results versus common opponents
  5. Overall won-lost record
  6. Adjusted scoring average
  7. Strength of schedule

Minimum Requirements

Important: Teams and individuals must play a minimum of 15 eighteen-hole stroke-play rounds, including a minimum of 8 stroke-play Division I rounds in the spring, to be considered for selection to the championships.

Equipment Rules

General Equipment Requirements

  • Balls and Clubs: Type of equipment shall be determined by the individual team and must be acceptable under Rules of Golf
  • One-Ball Rule: The one-ball rule (brand) will NOT be in effect
  • Golf Bags: Must have the name or logo of the school on them

Footwear

Mandatory: Players and coaches must use only soft-spike shoes during regional and championships competition, including all practice rounds.

Penalty for breach: Disqualification (for players) or misconduct (for coaches)

Electronic Devices

Coaches: May use electronic communication devices in a private manner provided that their use does not breach Rule 24-4a, Model Local Rule H-2 (Advice Allowed in Team Competition) or disrupt practice or play.

  • Ringers must be turned off
  • Violations will be considered misconduct

Distance-Measuring Devices: Allowed for players and coaches under Rule 14-3 during practice and competition, as long as they conform to USGA specifications.

Rules & Conduct Policies

NCAA Playing Rules

The NCAA Division I Women's Golf Championships shall be conducted according to the Rules of Golf and any local rules and conditions of competition implemented by the committee.

Advice Allowed in Team Competition

In accordance with Rule 24.4a, Model Local Rule H-2 has been adopted:

  • Two designated coaches may be appointed to give advice to members of their team at any time
  • A designated coach is defined as a member of the institution's coaching staff or an employee of the institution
  • Before the day's play, designated coaches must be identified to the games committee by signing in at the first assigned tee
  • Coaches must wear appropriate wristband (designated coach credential) at all times
  • Coaches are not caddies and are only granted the ability to give advice per Rules of Golf definition
  • Advice must be given in a private manner that does not disrupt play
  • Coach must not give advice to or ask for advice from another team's player or coaches

Misconduct Policy

Unsporting Conduct: A player (or coach) will be notified immediately on the golf course and provided a warning for unsporting conduct, including:

  • Willful acts of defacing the golf course
  • Misuse of electronic devices including iPods, cellular phones, etc.
  • Club throwing or breaking
  • Abusive language
  • Violation of the tobacco policy

Penalties:

  • Second occurrence in the same day: Player (or coach) will be disqualified for that round
  • Third occurrence in the championships: Disqualification from the championships
  • The NCAA committee is responsible for assessing the penalty

Uniform Requirements

  • Student-athletes must wear uniforms of the same color during practice and competition
  • Participants must wear appropriate golf attire at all times at the tournament site
  • The inseam of shorts must be at least four inches
  • Denim is not considered appropriate attire
  • Requirements apply to both regional and finals championships competition

Logo Guidelines

Logo guidelines apply to both regular-season and championships competition:

  • Athletics Equipment: Shoes, golf gloves, towels, backpacks, sling bags, rain caps, rainsuits, and umbrellas
  • Uniforms and Apparel: Socks, headbands, T-shirts, wristbands, belt buckles, and visors or hats
  • Maximum logo size: 2-1/4 square inches

Rule 1 – The Game, Player Conduct and the Rules

1.1 The Game of Golf

Golf is played in a round of 18 (or fewer) holes on a course by striking a ball with a club.

  • Each hole starts with a stroke from the teeing area
  • Ends when the ball is holed on the putting green
  • For each stroke: play the course as you find it and play the ball as it lies

1.2 Standards of Player Conduct

All players are expected to play in the spirit of the game by:

  • Acting with integrity – following the Rules, applying all penalties, being honest in all aspects of play
  • Showing consideration to others – playing at a prompt pace, looking out for safety, not distracting other players
  • Taking good care of the course – replacing divots, smoothing bunkers, repairing ball-marks, not causing unnecessary damage

Safety Warning: If a player plays a ball in a direction where there might be danger of hitting someone, they should immediately shout a warning, such as the traditional warning of "fore".

1.3 Playing by the Rules

Player Responsibility: Players are responsible for applying the Rules to themselves:

  • Players are expected to recognize when they have breached a Rule and to be honest in applying their own penalties
  • If a player knows they have breached a Rule that involves a penalty and deliberately fails to apply the penalty, the player is disqualified
  • Players may ask for help with the Rules from a referee or Committee

Penalty Levels

  • One-Stroke Penalty: Minor advantage or taking penalty relief
  • General Penalty: Loss of hole in match play, two-stroke penalty in stroke play
  • Disqualification: For serious misconduct or when advantage is too significant

Rule 3 – The Competition

3.2 Match Play

Winning a Hole: A player wins a hole when:

  • Player completes the hole in fewer strokes than the opponent
  • Opponent concedes the hole
  • Opponent gets the general penalty (loss of hole)

Tying a Hole: A hole is tied (halved) when both players complete in the same number of strokes

Winning a Match: Lead opponent by more holes than remain to be played

3.3 Stroke Play

In stroke play, all players compete based on total strokes.

  • The player who completes all rounds in the fewest total strokes is the winner
  • The marker keeps the player's score
  • After each hole, the marker confirms strokes and enters the gross score
  • Player must certify scores and return the scorecard promptly

Scoring Responsibilities

Marker's Responsibility:

  • Enter gross score for each hole
  • Certify hole scores after the round

Player's Responsibility:

  • Check hole scores entered by marker
  • Ensure marker certifies the scorecard
  • Certify hole scores and return scorecard promptly
  • Note: Player is NOT responsible for adding up scores or showing handicap

Rule 4 – The Player's Equipment

4.1 Clubs

Conforming Clubs: Players must use clubs that conform to Equipment Rules

14-Club Limit: A player must not:

  • Start a round with more than 14 clubs
  • Have more than 14 clubs during the round

Damaged Clubs: If a club is damaged during the round (except through abuse), the player may:

  • Continue to make strokes with the damaged club, or
  • Repair the club or replace it with another club

4.2 Balls

  • Must use a conforming ball in making each stroke
  • May get a conforming ball from anyone, including another player
  • Must not play a ball whose characteristics have been deliberately altered

4.3 Use of Equipment

Allowed Uses:

  • Getting distance or directional information (distance-measuring devices)
  • Getting weather information available from forecasts
  • Using plain gloves that meet Equipment Rules
  • Using equipment for general stretching

Not Allowed:

  • Measuring elevation changes
  • Using devices to get recommended club selection
  • Listening to music to eliminate distractions or help with swing tempo
  • Using training or swing aids during the round

Rule 5 – Playing the Round

5.2 Practice on Course

Match Play: Player may practice on the course before a round

Stroke Play: Player must not practice on the course before a round, except:

  • Practice putting/chipping on or near first teeing area
  • Practice on any practice area

5.3 Starting and Ending Round

  • Starting: Round starts when player makes stroke to start first hole
  • Player must start at (and not before) their starting time
  • Starting time is treated as exact (9:00 am means 9:00:00 am)
  • Penalty for late arrival: Disqualification (unless arrival is ≤5 minutes late, then general penalty on first hole)

5.6 Pace of Play

Players must not unreasonably delay play. When it is the player's turn:

  • It is recommended the player make the stroke in no more than 40 seconds
  • Usually the player should play more quickly than that

Ready Golf in Stroke Play: Players are encouraged to play out of turn in a safe and responsible way to help pace of play.

Rule 6 – Playing a Hole

6.1 Starting Play of a Hole

A player must start each hole by playing a ball from anywhere inside the teeing area.

  • Match Play: If played from outside teeing area, opponent may cancel the stroke
  • Stroke Play: General penalty (two strokes) and must correct by playing from inside teeing area

6.2 Playing Ball from Teeing Area

Ball may be teed or played from ground:

  • From a tee placed in or on the ground, or
  • From the ground itself

Ball is not in play until stroke is made:

  • Ball may be lifted or moved without penalty before the stroke
  • If teed ball falls off tee before stroke, it may be re-teed without penalty

6.3 Ball Used in Play of Hole

A player must hole out with the same ball played from the teeing area, except when:

  • That ball is lost or out of bounds, or
  • The player substitutes another ball (whether allowed or not)

Wrong Ball: A player must not make a stroke at a wrong ball

  • Match Play: Loss of hole
  • Stroke Play: Two penalty strokes and must correct the mistake

Rule 10 – Preparing for and Making a Stroke

10.1 Making a Stroke

In making a stroke:

  • The player must fairly strike the ball with the head of the club
  • Must not push, scrape or scoop the ball
  • If the club accidentally hits the ball more than once, it counts as only one stroke with no penalty

10.2 Advice and Help

Advice: Any verbal comment or action intended to influence a player in:

  • Choosing a club
  • Making a stroke
  • Deciding how to play during a hole or round

Who May Give Advice:

  • The player's caddie (if they have one)
  • Partner or partner's caddie (in team formats)
  • NCAA: Two designated advice givers per team

Asking for/Giving Wrong Advice: General penalty

Rule 17 – Penalty Areas

Relief Options for Ball in Penalty Area

When a ball is in a penalty area, the player may:

  1. Play as it lies (no penalty)
  2. Stroke-and-distance relief (one penalty stroke)
    • Play from where previous stroke was made
  3. Back-on-the-line relief (one penalty stroke)
    • Drop on line from hole through point where ball last crossed edge
    • Relief area is one club-length from where ball first touches ground
  4. Lateral relief (one penalty stroke, red penalty areas only)
    • Two club-lengths from where ball last crossed edge

Rule 18 – Stroke-and-Distance Relief; Ball Lost or Out of Bounds

18.2 Ball Lost or Out of Bounds

A ball is lost if not found in three minutes after search begins.

A ball is out of bounds when all of it is outside the course boundary.

Procedure: The player must take stroke-and-distance relief:

  • Add one penalty stroke
  • Play from where previous stroke was made

18.3 Provisional Ball

If the original ball might be lost or out of bounds, the player may play a provisional ball:

  • Must announce intention to play provisional ball before making stroke
  • Must be played before going forward to search for original ball
  • If original ball is found in bounds within three minutes, must play original ball
  • If original ball is not found or is out of bounds, provisional ball becomes ball in play
