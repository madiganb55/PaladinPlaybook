Principal Changes Introduced in the 2023 Rules of Golf

Applying Penalties to Multiple Breaches (Rule 1.3c(4))

The Rule has been amended so that determining whether breaches are related or unrelated is no longer part of its application, meaning there will be fewer instances where multiple penalties will be applied.

Handicap on Scorecard (Rule 3.3b(4))

A player is no longer required to show their handicap on their scorecard. The Committee is responsible for calculating the player's handicap strokes.

Club Repair or Replacement (Rule 4.1a(2))

A player may now replace a damaged club, provided the club has not been damaged through abuse.

Incorrectly Substituted Ball (Rule 6.3b(3))

The penalty for playing an incorrectly substituted ball has been reduced from the general penalty to one penalty stroke.

Back-on-the-Line Relief Procedure

The player is now required to drop on the line. The spot where the ball first touches the ground creates a relief area that is one club-length in any direction from that point.

NCAA Division I Men's Golf Championship Format

Regionals (May 12-14, 2025)

Six 54-hole regional tournaments determine the 30 qualifying teams and six individuals not on qualifying teams who advance to the finals.

  • Three regionals: 13 teams and 10 individuals
  • Three regionals: 14 teams and 5 individuals
  • Advancement: Top 5 teams and top individual not on advancing teams from each regional

Regional Sites (2025)

  • Amherst, Virginia – Poplar Grove Golf Course (Liberty University, host)
  • Auburn, Alabama – Auburn University Club (Auburn University, host)
  • Bremerton, Washington – Gold Mountain Golf Club (University of Washington, host)
  • Reno, Nevada – Montreux Golf and Country Club (University of Nevada, Reno, host)
  • Tallahassee, Florida – Seminole Legacy Golf Club (Florida State University, host)
  • Urbana, Illinois – Atkins Golf Club (University of Illinois, host)

Finals (May 23-28, 2025)

Location: Omni La Costa Resort & Spa, Carlsbad, California (University of Texas at Austin, host)

Stroke Play (72 holes)

  • All 30 teams and 6 individuals complete 54 holes
  • Top 15 teams and top 9 individuals advance for 4th round
  • 72-hole individual champion crowned after 4th round
  • Top 8 teams after 72 holes advance to match play

Match Play (Team Competition)

  • Quarterfinals: #1 vs #8, #2 vs #7, #3 vs #6, #4 vs #5
  • Five individual matches per team match
  • First team to win 3 points advances
  • Semifinals and finals determine national champion

Rule 1 – The Game, Player Conduct and the Rules

1.1 The Game of Golf

Golf is played on a course by striking a ball with a club. Each hole starts with a stroke from the teeing area and ends when the ball is holed on the putting green.

For each stroke, the player:

  • Plays the course as they find it, and
  • Plays the ball as it lies

1.2 Standards of Player Conduct

All players are expected to play in the spirit of the game by:

  • Acting with integrity – following the Rules, applying all penalties, and being honest
  • Showing consideration to others – playing at a prompt pace, looking out for safety
  • Taking good care of the course – replacing divots, smoothing bunkers, repairing ball-marks

1.3 Playing by the Rules

Players are responsible for applying the Rules to themselves. If a player knows they have breached a Rule that involves a penalty and deliberately fails to apply the penalty, the player is disqualified.

Rule 2 – The Course

Five Areas of the Course

  • General Area: The entire course except the four specific areas below
  • Teeing Area: Where the player starts each hole
  • Penalty Areas: Bodies of water and other defined areas
  • Bunkers: Prepared areas of sand
  • Putting Green: Specially prepared area for putting

Objects and Conditions

  • Loose Impediments: Natural objects not fixed or growing (leaves, stones, twigs)
  • Movable Obstructions: Artificial objects that can be moved
  • Immovable Obstructions: Artificial objects that cannot be moved
  • Abnormal Course Conditions: Animal holes, ground under repair, immovable obstructions, temporary water

Rule 3 – The Competition

3.2 Match Play

In match play, a player and opponent compete based on holes won, lost, or tied.

  • Winning a Hole: Complete the hole in fewer strokes than the opponent
  • Tying a Hole: Complete the hole in the same number of strokes (halved)
  • Winning a Match: Lead opponent by more holes than remain to be played

3.3 Stroke Play

In stroke play, all players compete based on total strokes.

  • The player who completes all rounds in the fewest total strokes is the winner
  • The marker keeps the player's score
  • After each hole, the marker confirms strokes and enters the gross score
  • Player must certify scores and return the scorecard promptly

Scoring Responsibilities

Marker's Responsibility:

  • Enter gross score for each hole
  • Certify hole scores after the round

Player's Responsibility:

  • Check hole scores entered by marker
  • Ensure marker certifies the scorecard
  • Certify hole scores and return scorecard promptly
  • Note: Player is NOT responsible for adding up scores or showing handicap

Rule 4 – The Player's Equipment

4.1 Clubs

Conforming Clubs: Players must use clubs that conform to Equipment Rules

14-Club Limit: A player must not:

  • Start a round with more than 14 clubs
  • Have more than 14 clubs during the round

Damaged Clubs: If a club is damaged during the round (except through abuse), the player may:

  • Continue to make strokes with the damaged club, or
  • Repair the club or replace it with another club

4.2 Balls

  • Must use a conforming ball in making each stroke
  • May get a conforming ball from anyone, including another player
  • Must not play a ball whose characteristics have been deliberately altered

4.3 Use of Equipment

Allowed Uses:

  • Getting distance or directional information (distance-measuring devices)
  • Getting weather information available from forecasts
  • Using plain gloves that meet Equipment Rules
  • Using equipment for general stretching

Not Allowed:

  • Measuring elevation changes
  • Using devices to get recommended club selection
  • Listening to music to eliminate distractions or help with swing tempo
  • Using training or swing aids during the round

Rule 5 – Playing the Round

5.2 Practice on Course

Match Play: Player may practice on the course before a round

Stroke Play: Player must not practice on the course before a round, except:

  • Practice putting/chipping on or near first teeing area
  • Practice on any practice area

5.3 Starting and Ending Round

  • Starting: Round starts when player makes stroke to start first hole
  • Player must start at (and not before) their starting time
  • Starting time is treated as exact (9:00 am means 9:00:00 am)
  • Penalty for late arrival: Disqualification (unless arrival is ≤5 minutes late, then general penalty on first hole)

5.6 Pace of Play

Players must not unreasonably delay play. When it is the player's turn:

  • It is recommended the player make the stroke in no more than 40 seconds
  • Usually the player should play more quickly than that

Ready Golf in Stroke Play: Players are encouraged to play out of turn in a safe and responsible way to help pace of play.

Rule 6 – Playing a Hole

6.1 Starting Play of a Hole

A player must start each hole by playing a ball from anywhere inside the teeing area.

  • Match Play: If played from outside teeing area, opponent may cancel the stroke
  • Stroke Play: General penalty (two strokes) and must correct by playing from inside teeing area

6.2 Playing Ball from Teeing Area

Ball may be teed or played from ground:

  • From a tee placed in or on the ground, or
  • From the ground itself

Ball is not in play until stroke is made:

  • Ball may be lifted or moved without penalty before the stroke
  • If teed ball falls off tee before stroke, it may be re-teed without penalty

6.3 Ball Used in Play of Hole

A player must hole out with the same ball played from the teeing area, except when:

  • That ball is lost or out of bounds, or
  • The player substitutes another ball (whether allowed or not)

Wrong Ball: A player must not make a stroke at a wrong ball

  • Match Play: Loss of hole
  • Stroke Play: Two penalty strokes and must correct the mistake

Rule 10 – Preparing for and Making a Stroke

10.1 Making a Stroke

In making a stroke:

  • The player must fairly strike the ball with the head of the club
  • Must not push, scrape or scoop the ball
  • If the club accidentally hits the ball more than once, it counts as only one stroke with no penalty

10.2 Advice and Help

Advice: Any verbal comment or action intended to influence a player in:

  • Choosing a club
  • Making a stroke
  • Deciding how to play during a hole or round

Who May Give Advice:

  • The player's caddie (if they have one)
  • Partner or partner's caddie (in team formats)
  • NCAA: Two designated advice givers per team

Asking for/Giving Wrong Advice: General penalty

Rule 17 – Penalty Areas

Relief Options for Ball in Penalty Area

When a ball is in a penalty area, the player may:

  1. Play as it lies (no penalty)
  2. Stroke-and-distance relief (one penalty stroke)
    • Play from where previous stroke was made
  3. Back-on-the-line relief (one penalty stroke)
    • Drop on line from hole through point where ball last crossed edge
    • Relief area is one club-length from where ball first touches ground
  4. Lateral relief (one penalty stroke, red penalty areas only)
    • Two club-lengths from where ball last crossed edge

Rule 18 – Stroke-and-Distance Relief; Ball Lost or Out of Bounds

18.2 Ball Lost or Out of Bounds

A ball is lost if not found in three minutes after search begins.

A ball is out of bounds when all of it is outside the course boundary.

Procedure: The player must take stroke-and-distance relief:

  • Add one penalty stroke
  • Play from where previous stroke was made

18.3 Provisional Ball

If the original ball might be lost or out of bounds, the player may play a provisional ball:

  • Must be played before going forward to search
