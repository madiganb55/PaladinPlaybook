Track Event Categories

Indoor Track Events

  • Sprints: 55/60 Meters, 200 Meters, 400 Meters
  • Hurdles: 55/60 Hurdles (Men: 1.067m / Women: 0.840m)
  • Middle Distance: 800 Meters, Mile, 1500 Meters
  • Distance: 3000 Meters, 5000 Meters
  • Relays: 4×400 Relay, Distance Medley Relay (1200-400-800-1600)

Outdoor Track Events

  • Sprints: 100 Meters, 200 Meters, 400 Meters
  • Hurdles: 100/110 Hurdles, 400 Hurdles
  • Middle Distance: 800 Meters, 1500 Meters
  • Distance: 3000 Steeplechase, 5000 Meters, 10,000 Meters
  • Relays: 4×100 Relay, 4×400 Relay

Starting Procedures

The Starter Has Complete Control

The starter has entire control of competitors when on their marks and during the start. Their decisions regarding false starts are final.

Starting Commands

For Races 500 Meters or Shorter:

  1. "On your marks" - Runners take position (crouched or standing), no part of body touches on or beyond starting line
  2. "Set" - All competitors assume full and final set position and remain motionless
  3. Gun fires - Race begins

For Races Longer than 500 Meters:

  1. Runners take position not more than 3 meters behind starting line
  2. "On your marks" - Runners advance to starting line
  3. When all competitors are steady and motionless (without hands touching ground), gun fires

False Starts

⚠️ Zero Tolerance Rule

Any competitor who false starts is immediately disqualified.

A false start occurs when a competitor commences starting motion after assuming set position but before the gun fires.

Exception: In Combined Events, first false start is a warning; second false start results in disqualification.

Lane Rules and Violations

Running in Lanes

In races run entirely in lanes (100m, 200m, 400m, hurdles, relay legs in lanes):

  • Competitors must stay within their assigned lane from start to finish
  • May run outside lane on straightaway without advantage if no other runner is obstructed

Lane Violations - Disqualification Results From:

  • Stepping on or over the lane line to the left with more than one step on any curve throughout the race
  • Not starting and finishing within assigned lane
  • Flagrantly impeding another runner

Running Without Lanes

In distance races (800m and longer) where runners break to the inside:

  • Runners may pass on either right or left side
  • May cut in front of another runner with one full stride clearance
  • Cannot use body or arm action to impede another runner
  • Cannot veer to impede a challenging runner

Important: Curb Rule

Stepping on or over the curb with more than one step anywhere on any curve throughout the race results in disqualification (unless pushed by another runner).

Hurdle Rules

Hurdle Heights

Event Men's Height
55/60 Hurdles (Indoor) 1.067m (42")
100/110 Hurdles (Outdoor) 1.067m (42")
400 Hurdles (Outdoor) 0.914m (36")

Hurdling Violations - Disqualification Results From:

  • Advancing or trailing a leg or foot outside the hurdle width and below the top plane of the hurdle
  • Not hurdling each hurdle within the competitor's lane
  • Deliberately knocking down any hurdle
  • Deliberately knocking down any hurdle by hand

Note: Accidentally knocking down a hurdle while properly clearing it is NOT a violation.

Relay Rules

Basic Relay Requirements

  • Four unique competitors of the same gender constitute a relay team
  • Each runner (except shuttle relays) must carry a baton
  • Baton must be 28-30 cm long, 12-13 cm circumference, minimum 50 grams

Exchange Zones

Sprint Relays (4×100, 4×200):

  • 30-meter exchange zone
  • Outgoing runners may place two markers on track as reference (not in exchange zone)
  • Markers cannot be longer than lane width or wider than 2 inches

Distance Relays (4×400, 4×800, etc.):

  • 20-meter exchange zone
  • No markers allowed on track
  • Outgoing runners must line up in same relative position as incoming teammates

Relay Violations - Disqualification Results From:

  • Baton passed outside the exchange zone
  • Last runner finishes without baton
  • Using gloves, tape, or adhesive on hands to enhance baton contact
  • Transporting baton in manner other than in the hand while running
  • Team member running more than one leg
  • Any team member is disqualified during the race

Dropped Baton Rule

Inside zone: Either runner may retrieve it.

Outside zone: Only the person who dropped it may retrieve it (may leave lane if no other runner impeded).

Timing and Finish

Placement at Finish

Runners are placed in order by when any part of their torso crosses the perpendicular plane of the finish line.

Torso defined as: Any part of body other than head, neck, arms, legs, hands, or feet.

Timing Requirements

  • Fully Automatic Timing (FAT) is required for all NCAA competitions
  • Times recorded to 1/100th of a second (next slower 1/100th)
  • Photo finish images must be available for review
  • Backup timing system required

Tiebreaking

For determining place or advancement, ties are broken by reading photo finish to 1/1000th of a second accuracy (within same race only).

Advancement and Qualifying

Heat Formation

  • Maximum of two rounds of competition before finals (for time qualifiers)
  • Heats formed by seeding based on entry performance marks
  • Top competitors seeded throughout heats to ensure competitive balance

Qualifying Methods

Competitors advance to next round based on:

  1. Place: First X finishers from each heat automatically advance
  2. Time: Next fastest times across all heats fill remaining spots

Tie for Last Qualifying Position

If lanes/positions available: All tying runners advance

If not enough lanes: Determined by runoff (or draw by lot if designated before meet)

Finals

  • Outdoor sprints/hurdles in lanes: Maximum 8-9 competitors (based on available lanes)
  • Indoor events: Based on venue lane availability
  • Distance races: 9-24 competitors depending on event

🌲 Cross Country Rules

Cross Country Overview

Cross country is run on natural terrain (fields, woods, grasslands) with courses designed for safety and fair competition.

Course Requirements

Course Lengths:

  • Men: At least 4000 meters (typically 8K or 10K)

Course Layout Standards:

  • Course must be at least 4 meters wide throughout
  • First turn at least 200 meters (preferably 400 meters) from start
  • Last 100 meters must be straight and at least 4 meters wide
  • Entire course clearly marked with continuous boundary markers
  • Must have kilometer/mile markings throughout
  • Avoid dangerous ascents/descents, deep ditches, and obstacles
  • Minimize continuous road traversing

Starting Procedures (Cross Country)

Starting Line:

  • Arc-shaped to ensure all runners equidistant from first turn
  • Wide enough to provide 50cm space per front-line runner
  • Boxes/positions numbered left to right facing course
  • Teams assigned to center boxes by random draw

Starting Commands:

  1. One whistle blast - "Runners to the line"
    • Starter holds arms straight out with pistol in one hand, flag in other
  2. "Runners set" - Pistol and flag raised slowly overhead
    • All runners must be steady
  3. Gun fires - Simultaneous pistol firing and flag pulling down

Recall Point

Race may be recalled for unfair start if incident occurs within 100 meters of start line and clearly associated with the start.

Cross Country Team Scoring

Team Composition:

  • Minimum of 5 runners required to score
  • Maximum of 12 runners allowed per team (or as agreed)
  • Only first 7 runners on team count for scoring

Scoring System:

  1. First 5 finishers on each team score points based on finish position
  2. Lower total score wins
  3. 6th and 7th runners are "displacers" - push opponents' scores higher
  4. Only runners from complete teams (5+ finishers) receive scoring places

Scoring Example

If Team A finishes: 1st, 3rd, 5th, 10th, 15th = 34 points

If Team B finishes: 2nd, 4th, 6th, 8th, 12th = 32 points

Team B wins (lower score)

Breaking Team Ties:

Compare place finish of 5 scoring members in order - team with majority of winning comparisons wins.

Cross Country Legal Running

  • Runners may pass on either right or left side
  • May cut in front with one full stride clearance
  • Cannot impede another runner's progress
  • Cannot leave course and return to continue race
  • Must complete prescribed course as marked

Cross Country Violations

Disqualification results from:

  • False start
  • Impeding another runner
  • Cutting in front without proper clearance
  • Voluntarily leaving course then returning
  • Not completing prescribed course
  • Forcing way between two runners with contact
  • Receiving assistance during race
  • Using wireless electronic device during competition

Cross Country Finish Procedures

  • Finish line 4-10 meters wide, perpendicular to course
  • Finish chute recommended for accurate placing
  • All starters recorded; finishers given overall place
  • DNF (Did Not Finish) recorded for non-finishers

Uniforms and Equipment

Uniform Requirements

  • Must wear official school-issued team uniform (top and bottom)
  • All team members must wear same color/combination on competition day
  • Top must cover full torso and meet/hang below waistband
  • Manufacturer logo limited to 2¼ square inches

Shoe Requirements (CRITICAL NEW RULES)

⚠️ 2025-2026 Shoe Regulations

All shoes must be on the World Athletics approved list

Track Events - Maximum sole thickness:

  • Track events under 800m: 20mm
  • Track events 800m and above: 20mm
  • Maximum 11 spikes per shoe
  • Spike length: 9mm (12mm for high jump)

Cross Country - Shoe regulations:

  • Spiked shoes: Maximum sole thickness 20mm
  • Non-spiked shoes: Maximum sole thickness 40mm
  • Maximum 6 spike positions per shoe
  • Spike length: Maximum 25mm
  • Spike diameter: 4mm maximum

Non-compliance results in immediate disqualification

Starting Blocks (Track Only)

  • May be used for races 500 meters or shorter
  • Must be rigid construction without artificial starting aids
  • Must be set within assigned lane without overlapping starting line

Protests and Appeals

Protest (Written)

  • Must be made in writing immediately
  • Not later than 15 minutes after results deemed official and time-stamped
  • Referee reviews all evidence including official video
  • Referee's decision communicated to affected coaches

Appeal

  • Only available if Jury of Appeals appointed before meet
  • Must be submitted in writing within 30 minutes of referee's decision
  • Jury of Appeals decision is final

Correctable Error

Within 72 hours after meet completion, results can be corrected without protest if administrative errors detected (incorrect calculation, timing errors, placing errors based on factual evidence).

Common Violations Summary

Track Events - Actions Resulting in Disqualification:

  • False start (any movement after "set" before gun)
  • Stepping on/over lane line to left with more than one step on curve
  • Stepping on/over curb with more than one step on curve
  • Not finishing in assigned lane (for in-lane races)
  • Impeding another runner
  • Cutting in front of runner without proper clearance
  • Leaving track and returning to race
  • Receiving assistance from coach/teammate during race
  • Hurdle violations (trail leg outside width, running around hurdle, etc.)
  • Relay: passing baton outside zone, finishing without baton
  • Wearing non-compliant shoes or uniform
  • Unsporting conduct

Cross Country - Actions Resulting in Disqualification:

  • False start
  • Impeding another runner
  • Cutting in front without proper clearance
  • Voluntarily leaving course and returning
  • Not completing prescribed course
  • Forcing way between runners with contact
  • Using wireless electronic device during race
  • Wearing non-compliant shoes or uniform
  • Unsporting conduct

Key Definitions

Fully Automatic Timing (FAT)
Timing using technology such as digital slit-cameras, started automatically by starting device
Heat
Single segment of competitors competing head-to-head for purpose of advancing to subsequent round
Section
Single segment of competitors competing for final placement and scoring (timed section final)
Break Line
Arc across track where competitors may leave lanes/alleys (800m, relay exchanges)
Staggers
Measurements used to ensure all competitors run same distance when starting in lanes around curves
Impede/Impeding
Preventing or obstructing progress of competitor along their established course
Torso
Any part of body other than head, neck, arms, legs, hands, or feet (used for finish placement)
DNF (Did Not Finish)
Runner who started but did not complete the race
